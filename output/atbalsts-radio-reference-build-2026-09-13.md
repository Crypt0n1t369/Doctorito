# Atbalsts radio/LoRa reference build and commissioning specification

13 September 2026 · **Reference design for a hypothetical Latvian pilot; no RF field validation yet**

This closes the engineering choices left open in the [architecture draft](atbalsts-bidirectional-audio-radio-lorawan-architecture-2026-09-13.md). It is a buildable **test configuration**, not a claim that any named site has coverage or that a broadcaster has agreed to carry the signal. Exact antenna geometry, radiated power, battery size and the final audio waveform are set by the acceptance rules below. Those values cannot be truthfully determined without a site and off-air measurements.

## Service envelope to design against

| Parameter | Reference pilot decision |
|---|---|
| Geography | One Latvian centre/broadcast site and two named staffed community stations, selected after a roof and indoor survey; no assumed kilometre radius |
| Outage | Public internet and cellular unavailable; centre, FM programme feed, transmitter, LoRa gateway and both stations powered for **72 hours** |
| Traffic | Twenty **128-byte synthetic compact reports per hour total**, ten per station, plus a small centre-issued public bulletin at least every five minutes in scheduled FM slots |
| Resident access | Preinstalled offline phone app; one ordinary battery FM radio for listening; residents submit digitally while physically near a station |
| Public authority | One centre key/area owner; operator review before publication; report inbox always unverified |
| Core performance target | At surveyed DR3/SF9-or-better sites, ≥95/100 complete public imports within two broadcast cycles and ≥95/100 complete centre receipts within 15 minutes under the stated load; report every failure and test condition |

The 95/100 targets are **pilot gates**, not a statistical certification of an emergency service. If a site cannot sustain DR3 in both required directions, it exits this performance envelope until the antenna/site changes or the receipt latency target is relaxed. A public bulletin must still include spoken instructions for people without the app.

## Reference physical deployment

For the first end-to-end test, put the **centre workstation and FM audio-injection terminal on the same protected LAN or in the same broadcaster facility**. This removes an otherwise untested centre→broadcaster WAN dependency. The centre's LoRaWAN server is on that protected LAN; an outdoor gateway is wired to it. A backup-powered second centre-to-studio feed is required if they are separated. The broadcaster must demonstrate that the programme feed **and transmitter** continue after mains and public-internet loss. [Latvian broadcaster/permit route](https://www.esakari.lv/lv/radioatlaujas).

Two stationary community boxes have local queues, audio capture/playback and LoRaWAN end devices. Their normal public downlink is the FM receiver and line-out (or resident radio speaker), avoiding a 1 KB LoRa downlink. LoRaWAN carries compact private report uplinks and centre receipts. Local Wi-Fi is the preferred routine phone→station transfer after its offline test passes; acoustic transfer remains the required independent demonstration/fallback. No bridge decodes report plaintext or turns a report into a public bulletin.

If a future regional hub must convert an FM audio object into LoRa, the centre issues a **separate small signed LoRa bulletin** that the hub can relay byte-for-byte. It must have a distinct type and limited semantics; a hub cannot extract part of a complete signed snapshot and claim it is complete. In reverse, a remote hub needs an independently licensed two-way radio/data backhaul, surviving wired path or satellite path to the centre. Public FM remains one-way.

An [RAK7289V2 can transmit up to 27 dBm in hardware](https://docs.rakwireless.com/product-categories/wisgate/rak7289v2/datasheet/); that is **not** the pilot transmit setting. Initial lab/field settings use the EU868 default 868.1, 868.3 and 868.5 MHz uplink channels, 125 kHz and **no more than 10 dBm conducted power with a low-gain antenna** until the RF engineer signs off the measured ERP. For 868–868.6 MHz in Latvia the relevant row sets 25 mW ERP and an alternative ≤1% duty-cycle route; the configured maximum must account for `conducted dBm + antenna gain in dBd − feeder loss`. Configure the legal sub-band duty tracker on every transmitter. The normal EU868 RX2 is 869.525 MHz at DR0/SF12; the proposed DR3/SF9 RX2 setting for fast receipts is sent through the LoRaWAN network and must be acknowledged and tested after power cycling. The 869.4–869.65 MHz row has different ERP/duty conditions; it does **not** make 500 mW the system default. [Latvian plan, rows 48/54](https://likumi.lv/ta/id/338729), [EU868 defaults](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/docs/lorawan/regional-parameters/eu868/), [RX2 parameter update](https://learn.semtech.com/mod/book/view.php?chapterid=141&id=173).

For a licensed private VHF/UHF radio backhaul, choose a radio pair and frequency *after* the authorized operator or [SIA Elektroniskie sakari](https://www.esakari.lv/lv/radioatlaujas) defines the service and assignment. The hardware must expose line audio, PTT control and a documented channel bandwidth. Pin [Dire Wolf AX.25/1200-baud AFSK](https://github.com/wb2osz/direwolf) as the reference packet modem for a suitable analogue FM voice channel; measure PTT preamble loss, squelch tails, packet error and legal airtime. Do not presume an amateur channel can carry private encrypted resident reports. If the licensed channel bandwidth or audio processing defeats AFSK, compare [Codec2/FreeDV VHF 2400B](https://github.com/drowe67/codec2/blob/main/README_freedv.md) on that exact channel and re-run the same acceptance tests.

### Backup power procurement rule

The electrical tender must quote **usable** watt-hours at the site's temperature and output mode, not only a nominal marketing capacity. Before ordering final batteries, log 24 hours of actual average/peak watts with Class C radio, FM receiver, Wi-Fi and loudspeaker active. Require `usable Wh ≥ measured average W × 72 h`, plus documented start-up/peak power and a safe automatic restart. As an initial quote envelope, request **1.5 kWh nominal per station** (supports only ~16.7 W average for 72 h at 80% usable) and **4 kWh nominal for the centre+gateway power domain** (supports only ~44.4 W average on the same assumption). Increase capacity or add a fuel-backed generator if measured draw exceeds those limits. The FM transmitter's power and fuel plan belongs to the broadcaster and must be tested separately. The gateway accepts regulated PoE or regulated 12 V; its vendor warns against uncontrolled solar-charger voltage. [RAK7289V2 power inputs](https://docs.rakwireless.com/product-categories/wisgate/rak7289v2/datasheet/).

These batteries bridge an outage; they do not solve the single-gateway or single-centre failure. A live resilience service needs a **second active gateway with overlapping surveyed coverage on an independent power path**, a tested standby centre/server and protected key restore, and a second programme-feed path. The initial one-active/one-spare pilot remains a reliability experiment.

## Complete pilot protocol profile

The bytes that cross the three media are **application objects**. Each medium has its own physical framing; a bridge never changes the application-object bytes. Public objects require a valid centre signature. Resident reports are encrypted on the phone and can be opened only by the centre. A correctly encrypted report is still an unverified claim.

| Object | Wire format and rule |
|---|---|
| `ATB` public snapshot | Existing deterministic-CBOR COSE_Sign1/Ed25519 profile, ≤1,024 bytes, centre/area/registry/epoch/sequence/issued/expires bound into the signed payload. The exact implemented pilot schema remains [here](atbalsts-radio-security-proof/protocol-spec.md). Only operator-approved committed state may be signed. |
| `ATR1` rich report | Existing 314-byte HPKE Base envelope and ≤160 UTF-8-byte note, retained for Wi-Fi, faster RF or delayed store-and-forward. [Existing profile](atbalsts-radio-security-proof/protocol-spec.md). |
| **Proposed `ATR2` compact report** | Exactly **128 bytes**: 10-byte header = 4 ASCII bytes `ATR2` + 4-byte centre decryption-key ID + 2-byte padded-plaintext length `0x0046`; 32-byte HPKE encapsulation; 70-byte padded plaintext encrypted with a 16-byte AES-GCM tag. Use the same HPKE suite as `ATR1` with new domain-separated `info = "atbalsts/report/v2" || 0x00 || header`. Plaintext: 2-byte big-endian canonical-CBOR length, then deterministic CBOR `[2, areaId, observedAtUnixSeconds, category, severity, latitudeE5, longitudeE5, note]`, then zero padding. `areaId/time` are uint32; category `0=access,1=hazard,2=shelter,3=other`; severity `0=unknown,1=low,2=medium,3=urgent`; coordinates are signed 32-bit `degrees × 100,000` within legal latitude/longitude bounds; note ≤40 UTF-8 bytes with no control characters. Time and location are unverified reporter claims, and the offline app must support manual location selection. The largest allowed CBOR example encodes to 66 bytes, so 2-byte length + body fits within 70. Reject any invalid field, noncanonical CBOR or nonzero pad. **This is specified, not yet implemented or independently reviewed.** |
| **Proposed `ATR-RECEIPT1`** | Centre signs deterministic CBOR payload `[1, first16bytes(SHA256(reportEnvelope)), committedAtUnixSeconds]` as tagged COSE_Sign1/Ed25519, with protected `kid` 4 bytes, empty unprotected map and external AAD `atbalsts/receipt/v1`. Its maximum size is **105 bytes** with uint32 time. The 128-bit report-hash prefix is only a receipt locator; the centre stores the full SHA-256 hash, rejects any different full hashes with the same prefix, and the phone/station matches the prefix to its original report. Issue only *after* the encrypted envelope is durably committed to the private inbox. This receipt does not attest truth, review or dispatched help. **Specified, not implemented.** |

The compact report and receipt sizes were checked against the existing project's canonical-CBOR encoder with [a reproducible size fixture](atbalsts-radio-security-proof/reference-sizes.mjs); the calculations should become independent interoperability fixtures before RF tests. The centre keeps separate signing and report-decryption keys. The station and regional hub get only the appropriate public keys, LoRaWAN credentials and opaque queues. Phones must already contain the public signing and HPKE-recipient keys. [COSE](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9052.html), [HPKE](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9180.html).

### Transport framing, retries and receipt return

1. **Audio baseline `M0`:** use the present Atbalsts audio modem at 8 kHz, PCM16, 128-byte frames carrying 112 application-object bytes, with two scheduled repetitions. Each burst has its own sync and frame CRC. This is the *initial implementation choice* because it has one real phone-speaker → laptop-mic success and clean software round trips; it has **no off-air FM evidence**. [Local measurement](atbalsts-radio-review/review.md). Save exact sender WAVs and a manifest.
2. **Audio challenger `M1`:** benchmark the [Rattlegram modem](https://github.com/aicodix/rattlegram) at its 128-byte mode on the identical encrypted/signed objects. Its published short-packet performance is not an Atbalsts FM result. Whichever waveform reaches the full-object success gate across both phone OS families and real FM radio speakers becomes the pinned field mode; if neither passes, try the slower 85-byte profile or a narrow robust mode. Do not combine unrelated audio waveforms in one unannounced broadcast slot.
3. **LoRaWAN:** private EU863–870 cell; matched server/device MAC profile; unique OTAA keys; fixed-site ADR after survey; Class C for externally powered stations; no routine confirmed uplinks. Gateway connects by local authenticated [Basics Station TLS](https://www.chirpstack.io/docs/chirpstack-gateway-bridge/backends/basics-station.html) to ChirpStack. After join, station sends an uplink to enable Class C scheduling; a rejoin and RX2 DR change must be exercised with power interruptions. [Class C](https://www.thethingsindustries.com/docs/hardware/devices/configuring-devices/class-c/).
4. **Fragmented objects:** FPort 10, 8-byte header: version/type (one byte), 32-bit transfer ID, 8-bit zero-based index, 8-bit fragment count, reserved-zero byte. Allocate transfer IDs as a durable **monotonic counter per sending device**, reuse the same ID on retries and rotate the device epoch before counter wrap. Receiver keys reassembly by origin device identity + transfer ID, caps object length by type, rejects conflicting duplicates, concatenates in index order and hashes the complete bytes. Fragmentation uses the *current* permitted application payload size after any MAC options, never a hard-coded 51/115/222-byte assumption. The LoRaWAN MIC protects link frames; only COSE/HPKE authenticates the whole application object. Centre deduplicates the full report hash.
5. **Whole-object receipt fast path:** if the current LoRaWAN downlink application budget can hold the **105-byte** receipt, send its original COSE bytes on FPort 11 with no fragment header; otherwise use FPort 10 fragmentation. At DR3 with no MAC options the reference maximum is 115 application bytes, giving 10 bytes of margin. A MAC command can reduce this budget; the server must check the actual frame. At DR0 the receipt needs three 8-byte-header fragments. [EU868 payload table](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/docs/lorawan/regional-parameters/eu868/).
6. **Retry and power-loss rules:** station acknowledges only *local durable copy* to the phone. Upload fragments as unconfirmed messages with legal duty pacing and jitter. If no centre receipt arrives five minutes after the last fragment at the surveyed DR3 core, retry the same full object/transfer ID with exponential backoff and a per-station airtime quota. The centre accepts duplicates idempotently. If a receipt downlink is missed, station requests the signed receipt by the full 32-byte report hash in a short uplink; centre resends the original receipt. Every queue and LoRaWAN counter survives process restart. Expired reports remain labelled unsent/late; never fabricate “centre received.” [Confirmed-uplink tradeoff](https://www.thethingsindustries.com/docs/hardware/devices/concepts/confirmed-uplinks-behavior/).

For the reference implementation, store station outboxes and the centre's private inbox in SQLite on SSD with `journal_mode=WAL` and `synchronous=FULL`. The station's “I have a copy” response follows a committed queue transaction; the centre's signed receipt follows a committed inbox transaction with a unique full SHA-256 report ID. Keep the exact envelope, transfer ID, attempts, timestamps and receipt bytes. Reserve at least **1,000 queue slots per station**: 72 hours × 10 reports/hour = 720 reports before any retry or outage backlog, and the extra slots give a modest margin. A full queue refuses new digital intake visibly instead of dropping an acknowledged report. Verify the storage device's actual power-loss behavior; software settings alone do not repair a faulty SSD. [SQLite WAL durability with FULL sync](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_synchronous).

### Capacity check for the chosen profile

The separate [private-channel and network-capacity assessment](atbalsts-private-radio-throughput-2026-09-13.md) extends this initial report-only check to FM private messages, gateway downlinks, group-key fanout and a licensed-radio comparison.

The existing [reproducible airtime calculator](atbalsts-radio-eu868-airtime.py) assumes 125 kHz, CR 4/5, an 8-symbol preamble, no extra MAC options and a single sub-band; it is an analytic **best case** before losses. At DR3/SF9, a 128-byte report takes **two uplink fragments and 0.96 seconds of radio transmission**. Ten reports/hour/station consume about 9.6 seconds of a 36-second/hour 1% uplink budget; reserve the remainder for joins, retries and control. At DR0/SF12, that same report needs three fragments and 8.38 seconds of airtime, making the 20/hour service envelope impossible across two stations on that sub-band. The 314-byte `ATR1` report is worse at DR0. [EU868 reference limits](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/docs/lorawan/regional-parameters/eu868/), [Latvian sub-band rules](https://likumi.lv/ta/id/338729).

The 105-byte signed receipt at DR3/SF9 can use **one** unfragmented downlink with about **0.64 seconds** airtime if its current MAC budget allows. At the default DR0 RX2, it takes **three** fragmented downlinks and about **7.56 seconds** total airtime; on one 10% sub-band, those three have roughly a minute of best-case transmit spacing, and twenty receipts/hour consume ~151 seconds of gateway transmit time before retries. A transmitting gateway is normally deaf to station uplinks. Therefore the **15-minute receipt gate depends on a measured, acknowledged DR3 RX2 path** and controlled downlink load; if that fails, the pilot must change the receipt SLA, add gateway coverage, or batch receipts. Do not call a LoRaWAN link ACK a centre receipt. [Gateway half-duplex limitation](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/docs/lorawan/limitations/), [EU868 RX2 default](https://www.thethingsnetwork.org/docs/lorawan/regional-parameters/eu868/).

## Commissioning plan that fixes the remaining site choices

| Gate | Required action and record | Pass / decision |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Agreements and bench | Written FM slot/feed/power agreement; site roof/antenna permissions; RF engineer reviews ERP; any private radio assignment confirmed. Build locally into a dummy load and audio loopback first. | No over-the-air trial until the licensed operator/authority authorizes it. [Latvian radio permissions](https://www.esakari.lv/lv/radioatlaujas) |
| 1. Energy | Record 24-hour average/peak watts per site, then run 72 hours with mains/public internet/cellular off, including one cold restart. | Actual usable Wh meets the 72-hour formula; FM feed and transmitter remain on; no committed queue item or trusted clock state is lost. |
| 2. Antenna and RF | Survey both station locations in their final indoor/external antenna positions; test multiple roof/antenna placements and log gateway ID, RSSI, SNR, DR, packet errors, retries, downlink RX2 acknowledgment and off-air interference. | Select the lowest legal ERP and antenna position that sustain **DR3 uplink and DR3 RX2** at both core sites under the stated traffic. If not, move/raise antenna or add an independently powered second gateway and repeat; do not claim the 15-minute SLA at a DR0-only site. |
| 3. Audio | Repeat M0 and M1 across 2 Android + 2 iPhone models, 0.2/1/2 m from ordinary radio speakers, late start, quiet/noisy room, low phone battery and FM line-out. Record injected and off-air WAVs, waveform gain/clipping, complete-object signature results and elapsed time. | Pin one waveform and level only if ≥95/100 *complete authenticated objects* arrive within two broadcast cycles in every declared core condition. If no mode passes, redesign audio or narrow the declared use envelope. |
| 4. Full loop | Disconnect public networks; phone sends a **synthetic** encrypted `ATR2`; station queues, LoRa relays and centre commits; receipt returns; operator reviews and signs a distinct public bulletin; FM reaches another offline phone. Inject queue-full, station/gateway/centre power cuts, duplicates, stale/forged packets and delayed receipt. | ≥95/100 centre receipts within 15 minutes at the reference load, zero false “centre received” states, zero forged public-state commits, and recovery after every defined interruption. Record failures, not just a successful demo. |
| 5. Readiness decision | Publish exact hardware/firmware, antenna height/loss/ERP, channel plan, power and RF logs, audio recordings, test manifest, failed runs, coverage map and operating procedure. | The documented performance claim names only sites and conditions actually passed. A live service requires overlapping active gateway coverage, centre failover and longer multi-site trials. |

Use one row per attempt in the [field log template](atbalsts-radio-field-trial-template.csv). The first funding milestone should buy *evidence*: installed but reversible pilot equipment, a reproducible protocol implementation, off-air recordings, verified end-to-end reports/receipts and a transparent failure report. No site-independent “works during any blackout” claim follows from this design alone.
