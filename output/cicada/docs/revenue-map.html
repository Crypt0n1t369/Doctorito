Groundwork

What a PA announcement actually is

The public-address system is the loudspeakers bolted to the ceiling of a station concourse, an airport pier, a hospital corridor, a ferry deck, a shopping centre. A PA announcement is anything spoken through them: the platform has changed, the train is late, the lift is broken, please leave the building.

Two things about them matter commercially. The first is that they are already installed everywhere — this is the one piece of infrastructure a venue does not have to buy. The second is that they are not free text. They are a small set of sentence templates with a few values filled in:

"Train {service} to {destination} now departs from platform {platform}, not platform {wasPlatform}, {reason}."

Sync that sentence to phones in every language the venue serves, and the broadcast only has to carry the template number and five values. Eleven bytes. It stays eleven bytes whether the station publishes in two languages or twelve, because none of the text is on the air.

A spoken platform change occupies the PA for six to eight seconds, in one language. The same announcement as data is 0.55 seconds unsigned, or 1.2 seconds signed, in four languages at once — and a passenger who cannot hear it reads it on their phone instead.

Buildable now

Four streams that need no new science

built

Accessible passenger information

€2–8k per site per year

The announcement as large text on every phone in the concourse, in the reader's language, offline, with a haptic alert for anything urgent. Runs over speakers the venue already owns. Replaces nothing and duplicates nothing — it reaches the people the PA never did.

Needs · one measured PA system, and the obligation confirmed in writing per country.
Against · nothing competes directly. Induction loops solve a narrower problem at higher cost.
near

Resilient public warning

€150–500k per country

Cell broadcast is the correct primary channel and is already mandated. This is the second path for when the grid or the network is the thing that failed: FM stays up on generators, people still have radios and phones, and a phone cannot hear RDS. Sold as the fallback that gets exercised in drills — never as a replacement.

Needs · a lighthouse civil-protection buyer and one live exercise with published results.
Against · cell broadcast whenever the network is up. Concede that first or lose the room.
near

Air-gapped data ingress

€25–150k per site per year

Configuration, attestations and key material into machines with no network, currently moved on USB sticks. An acoustic path is one-way by construction, cannot exfiltrate without a speaker, and is audible — so it is auditable by anyone standing in the room.

Against · many security teams classify any acoustic channel as a threat. Expect to argue that a sanctioned, audible, logged channel beats an unmonitored one more than once.
near

Arm's-length device provisioning

per-device royalty

Commissioning headless sensors and controllers that have a microphone but no screen, keypad or network. The one place near-ultrasonic earns its keep, since the path is direct playback under a metre.

Needs · an embedded C port of the modem. The JavaScript one is a prototype, not a product.
Against · NFC and QR are cheaper whenever the device has a screen or a person has a camera.
The agentic turn

Agents are about to get microphones, and nothing tells them what to believe

Two things are becoming true at once. Assistants are moving onto devices that listen continuously, and they are being given the authority to act — to rebook a train, to move a meeting, to warn someone. That combination creates a problem nobody has a product for: a microphone is an input channel with no authentication on it whatsoever.

Anything audible in a room is, to an agent, indistinguishable from an instruction. A voice on a speaker saying "the platform has changed to eleven" and a person standing behind you saying the same thing are the same bytes. As synthetic speech becomes free, that stops being a theoretical concern.

A signed channel is the missing primitive. Cicada already emits announcements that carry a venue's Ed25519 signature, bound to the channel they were issued for, verifiable offline against a pinned key. That is exactly the shape of "this instruction genuinely came from the operator of this building, and not from someone standing next to you."
18–24 mo

Local event bus for on-device agents

platform

An agent in a station today would have to scrape a website to learn the platform changed. An agent that can hear gets the same fact as a signed, structured, typed event — template 1, service 2041, platform 11 — with no API key, no partnership, no rate limit and no integration deal. The venue publishes once for its passengers; every agent in earshot reads it for free.

Needs · nothing new in the modem. A documented event schema, a public trust directory, and an SDK aimed at assistant platforms rather than at web pages.
Against · the same fact is usually available over an API, when the agent has a network and the venue has an API. This wins in the gap: no signal, no integration, or no time.
24–36 mo

The agent trust layer

platform

Not "how does an agent hear data" but "what is an agent permitted to act on from its microphone". A policy layer that says: treat audio as data, never as instructions, unless it carries a signature from a key this device has pinned. Sold to assistant platforms and to enterprises deploying agents in physical space.

Needs · the installed base from the streams above. A trust layer with nothing signing into it is a specification, not a product.
Against · platforms may decide their own attestation scheme, or decide the problem is theirs. Being first with real deployments is the only defence.
research

Acoustic provenance

€100k+ · media, legal, insurance

File-level provenance breaks the moment somebody films a screen. A signature carried in the sound itself survives re-recording, which is the one path that defeats every container-based scheme. "This audio was genuinely spoken at this event, at this time, by this authority" — verifiable from a phone recording of it.

Needs · real research. The carrier must be inaudible and must ride underneath speech rather than taking its own time slot, which is psychoacoustic masking, not this modem.
Against · an entrenched audience-measurement industry already does the hard part. Partner rather than rebuild.
Unstable times

Two streams that exist because infrastructure is being attacked

Resilience budgets are growing and, unusually, they are procurement-ready: the Critical Entities Resilience Directive and NIS2 both push named, tested fallbacks for essential services, which turns "what if this fails" from a conversation into a line item.

12 mo

Presence attestation without GPS

€30–90k per site

Satellite positioning is being jammed and spoofed routinely across the Baltic and Black Sea regions, and a great deal of "this person was here" evidence rests on it. A signed, timestamped broadcast that a device can only have heard by being physically in the room is a different kind of proof, and it does not depend on a satellite, a network or a map. Shift attendance in critical facilities, chain of custody, safety inductions.

Needs · a rolling nonce in the payload so a recording cannot be replayed later. The protocol deliberately does not decide freshness policy today; this stream requires that it does.
Against · replay is the whole attack. Until the freshness design is done and reviewed, this is not sellable.
opportunistic

Humanitarian field messaging

grant-funded

Reception centres, border crossings and field operations where the population speaks several languages, the network is saturated or absent, and a loudspeaker already exists. The multilingual template layer is the whole product; it needs no modification.

Against · grant-funded, slow, and low margin. Worth doing for the reference and the goodwill, not for the revenue.
The answer

Accessibility now, the agent trust layer later, and they are the same deployment

Short term — the next twelve months

Accessible passenger information, and nothing else. It is the only stream where the product is built, the buyer is identifiable, the budget already exists because of a deadline that has passed rather than one that is coming, and no competitor occupies the ground. Every other stream on this map is improved by having one reference customer here, and none of them is improved by starting early.

The work is not engineering. It is one measured PA system, one written legal opinion on the obligation, and one station that will let you fit it.

Long term — years two and three

The agent trust layer, built on the installed base that the accessibility business puts in the ground. This is the part worth being deliberate about now, because it changes nothing about what you build this year and everything about how you describe it.

Every station fitted for deaf passengers is, at no extra cost, a signed structured event source that any agent in earshot can read. The same 0.55-second broadcast serves both. One is a compliance purchase with a deadline; the other is a platform position that cannot be bought at any price without the first one existing. That is the reason to do the boring regulated business first, and it is the only reason that matters.

What not to do

  • Retail proximity marketing. Tried, low value, and it is the use case that earned ultrasonic data-over-sound its regulatory reputation.
  • Consumer file transfer. AirDrop and Quick Share are better at it and free.
  • Payments. NFC wins and the compliance burden is disproportionate.
  • Chasing the agent platforms first. A trust layer with nothing signing into it is a specification. Deployments come first, in that order, deliberately.
