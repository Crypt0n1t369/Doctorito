# Round 1 research: volunteer

23 September 2026. Hypotheses: VALUE-HYPOTHESES.md in this folder. Source kinds: docs / independent / marketing; pain strength: data / practitioner / anecdote / vendor. Raw agent output, not edited.

## V3 — partly_served
GAP: I found no tool that reads free-text offers arriving by WhatsApp, email, SMS or phone and turns them into commitments against a live needs list with a specific reply. Mainstream tools use forms and shifts. Where they have AI, it either helps the admin (Rosterfy) or recommends from the catalogue (Golden). The gap matters only in surges where offers vary a lot (goods, machinery, skills, housing) and arrive on channels nobody controls. The documented main cause of unused offers is missing written needs, limited capacity to absorb help, and vetting, and free-text parsing does not fix any of these. What it can realistically add is a fast, honest answer to every offer and one deduplicated ledger. Not checked (search budget ran out): EU civil-protection (UCPM) guidance, Latvian arrangements, and independent no-show rates.
  - [docs] Better Impact Volunteer Impact (now also owns Galaxy Digital Get Connected): Application forms with qualifications and interests. The admin filters candidates for a shift, then emails or texts the selected people and assigns them by hand. No reading of free-text offers is documented. <https://support.betterimpact.com/en/articles/9611716-managing-assignments-schedule-volunteers>
  - [marketing] Better Impact / Galaxy Digital merger: Combines Volunteer Impact, Get Connected (400+ volunteer centres) and Volunteer Link. The announcement mentions no AI and no free-text intake. <https://www.betterimpact.com/better-together>
  - [marketing] Rosterfy (Pip AI agent): Pip helps admins only: it creates, clones and edits events, shifts and forms. Its FAQ says it currently supports only event and shift management, so it does not read inbound volunteer messages. <https://www.rosterfy.com/platform/ai-agent-pip/>
  - [marketing] Golden: AI recommends opportunities from the organisation's own catalogue, and an AI coach supports volunteers (July 2025). Offers still start from the catalogue, not from the volunteer's own words. <https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/golden-unveils-ai-powered-app-update-designed-to-reverse-declining-volunteer-and-donor-engagement-302498921.html>
  - [marketing] SignUpGenius: People claim predefined slots on a sign-up sheet, and reminders go out automatically. <https://www.signupgenius.com/>
  - [marketing] VolunteerLocal: Registration forms, jobs and shifts, and automated email and SMS messages. <https://www.volunteerlocal.com/>
  - [marketing] Timecounts: Volunteers self-schedule into events and shifts. Messaging runs through a team inbox with bulk email. <https://join.timecounts.app/>
  - [independent] Crisis Cleanup: Volunteer phone operators type survivor requests from a hotline into a shared work-order database. Only vetted, affiliated organisations can claim the work. It handles needs, not offers. <https://teamrubiconusa.org/news-and-stories/hurricane-helene-how-to-get-help-assist-survivors/>
  - [marketing] NeedsList / RespondLocal: Says it collects and matches needs and offers in real time and has 750 partners. The homepage does not say how matching works. <https://needslist.co/>
  - [marketing] FEMA / Aidmatrix National Donations Management Network: State portals where people can offer money, goods, skills or time to disaster nonprofits. Whether it is still active is unclear from the page. <https://aidmatrixnetwork.org/fema/>
  - [independent] Ad hoc crisis stack (Valencia DANA 2024): A registration web form, plus free bus seats booked through a ticketing site. More than 14,000 volunteers were placed on buses this way. <https://www.elespanol.com/alicante/20241107/coordinan-voluntarios-ayudar-dana-valencia-nadie-vaya-propios-medios/899160239_0.html>
  PAIN:
  * [data] In a survey of 255 spontaneous volunteers (Cottrell 2010), 65.2% said their offers were never used. People who registered and were never contacted report feeling disappointed and undervalued. <https://knowledge.aidr.org.au/media/2140/national-spontaneous-volunteer-strategy.pdf>
  * [practitioner] FEMA and Points of Light guidance says unaffiliated volunteers' offers of help are often underused and can even be a problem for responders. It frames this as a gap between people's willingness and the system's capacity to use them. <https://www.fema.gov/pdf/donations/ManagingSpontaneousVolunteers.pdf>
  * [practitioner] Ahr flood 2021 (Helfer-Shuttle): survivor requests came in on paper, including a biscuit packet, by smartphone and by email. Organisers first scheduled 2,000 helpers on paper and later in Excel, and assigned people by calling out 'who is an electrician?'. About 100,000 helper-days were shuttled in by November 2021. <https://doku.iab.de/regional/RPS/2023/regional_rps_0123.pdf>
  * [data] A survey of more than 2,500 flood volunteers found they organised over Facebook and WhatsApp. Over 90% wanted a digital platform for exchanging needs and resources, and cooperation with official relief organisations was rare. <https://www.uni-wuppertal.de/de/news/detail/flutkatastrophe-2021-studie-gibt-einblicke-in-gedanken-von-freiwillig-helfenden/>
  * [practitioner] Superbergamo mutual aid (COVID): the group moved from phone to WhatsApp to a shared Excel sheet. The chat became unmanageable once it had more than 50 members, and the single operator was overwhelmed, so the role was split into two. <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2022.787202/full>
  * [data] #UnterkunftUkraine: about 360,000 free beds were offered, and more than 60,000 people had been placed by September 2022. <https://www.betterplace-lab.org/unterkunftukraine-erfolge-und-herausforderungen>
  * [anecdote] One host registered on 9 March and never heard back. The operator admitted a backlog and said placements were not tracked at first. <https://report-vor-ort.de/unterkunft-ukraine-hunderttausende-schlafplaetze-bleiben-ungenutzt/245045/>
  * [anecdote] Valencia DANA 2024: 15 volunteers were told to go elsewhere, and a municipality asked people to pause sending material. <https://www.elindependiente.com/espana/2024/11/06/la-ayuda-masiva-y-la-falta-de-organizacion-en-valencia-provocan-el-caos/>
  * [practitioner] Unwanted material donations pile up faster than useful supplies can be distributed (the 'second disaster'). Nelan, Penta and Wachtendorf (2019) recommend communication systems that tie offers to actual needs. <https://hazards.colorado.edu/research-counts/the-disaster-cycle/avoiding-the-second-disaster-of-unwanted-donations>
  * [anecdote] NHS Volunteer Responders: about 600,000 approved volunteers against about 250,000 tasks by June 2020, with complaints of more than 300 hours on duty and no task. Here the bottleneck was demand, not intake. Source is a search snippet; the paywalled article was not opened. <https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-daily-telegraph/20200601/281805696146654>
  * [data] Counter-evidence for routine volunteering: people who enquired but did not go ahead cited time (21%), no opportunity matching their skills (14%) and inflexibility (14%). The way the offer was made was not the barrier. <https://www.ncvo.org.uk/news-and-insights/news-index/time-well-spent-2023/volunteering-barriers-enablers/>
  * [practitioner] No evidence found for the claim that 'one coordinator holds 30 to 50 relationships'. Energize says there is no standard ratio; the examples it gives are CASA at 1 supervisor per 30 volunteers and SF Parks at 1 per 15. <https://www.energizeinc.com/a-z/book-excerpt/11739>
  * [vendor] A vendor-blog opinion (Fryar) proposes 1 manager per 80 volunteers, with about 30 active at any one time. It is not research. <https://blog.betterimpact.com/en/need-a-volunteer-database>
  * [vendor] No-shows: the only figure found is a vendor claim, without citation, that reminders cut no-shows by 30–50%. No independent no-show rates were found. <https://volunteerhub.com/blog/volunteer-coordinators-guide-to-reclaiming-time-and-balance>
  * [data] Research is starting on assigning up to 10,000 spontaneous volunteers, based on the Halle 2013 flood. It treats this as structured optimisation, not free-text intake. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09218>

## V5 — partly_served
GAP: Within one organisation, targeted asks by region, qualification or segment are standard. Two things are missing: asks based on capability described in free text (equipment, certificates or languages that are not in fixed fields), and consent and cooldown rules that hold across organisations. I found no evidence that anyone reports over-asking, broadcast fatigue or missing cooldowns as a problem.
  - [docs] Better Impact: Filters volunteers by qualifications, availability and interests, then emails or texts the selected people. <https://support.betterimpact.com/en/articles/9611716-managing-assignments-schedule-volunteers>
  - [marketing] Rosterfy: Segments volunteers and auto-syncs chat channels to shifts, roles, groups and locations for targeted messages. <https://www.rosterfy.com/platform/advanced-scheduling/volunteer-messaging/>
  - [docs] Team Österreich (Austrian Red Cross): Members pre-register online and are assigned to a local office. They get an SMS alert and reply Ja or Nein, and details follow by email. The page mentions no skill profiling. <https://www.roteskreuz.at/ich-will-helfen/team-oesterreich>
  - [marketing] British Red Cross Community Reserve Volunteers: People sign up quickly online and are called out by text when an emergency hits their area. The target was 10,000 reservists. <https://www.civilsociety.co.uk/voices/simon-lewis-how-community-reserve-volunteers-allow-us-to-mobilise-kindness.html>
  - [independent] Catchafire: Nonprofits post projects from more than 150 templates and the platform brings vetted skilled volunteers to them. The average match takes about 5 days. <https://www.humanserviceforum.org/catchafire-101-for-nonprofits/>
  - [marketing] Golden: AI recommends opportunities to each volunteer based on interests, skills and availability. <https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/golden-unveils-ai-powered-app-update-designed-to-reverse-declining-volunteer-and-donor-engagement-302498921.html>
  PAIN:
  * [data] 41.2% of US volunteers got involved with their main organisation because someone asked them, and 41.6% approached it themselves (BLS, 2015). Asking works, but this is personal asking by people they know. <https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/volun_02252016.htm>
  * [data] Among UK non-volunteers, 15% said being asked directly would encourage them, against 30% for flexible commitment. <https://www.ncvo.org.uk/news-and-insights/news-index/time-well-spent-2023/volunteering-barriers-enablers/>
  * [anecdote] No evidence found that broadcast callouts to reserve pools perform worse than capability-targeted asks, or that coordinators ask for consent and cooldown controls. <https://www.roteskreuz.at/ich-will-helfen/team-oesterreich>

## APPLICATIONS

- Surge offer desk: answer every offer during a flood or storm [medium] (V3, V7, V4)
  WHO: The 1–3 staff at a municipal volunteer centre or civil-protection commission (for example a US Volunteer Reception Center manager under ESF-15) handling thousands of phone, email and social-media offers of people, machinery and goods in the first two weeks
  TODAY: Hotline, registration web form, spreadsheets, Facebook and WhatsApp groups, ticketed bus seats (Valencia), paper and whiteboard (Ahr)
  SHORT: Offers vary a lot and arrive faster than anyone can reply. 65% of surveyed spontaneous volunteers said their offer was never used, and people who registered and were never contacted feel undervalued.
  VALUE: Every offer gets a specific answer in seconds: a slot, or 'not needed, here is what is open'. One deduplicated ledger replaces the spreadsheets. Someone must still write and update the needs list.

- In-kind goods intake against a live needs list [medium] (V3, V4)
  WHO: A Red Cross branch, food-bank or refugee-reception logistics lead receiving unsolicited offers of goods by phone, email and social media
  TODAY: Public needs lists on websites and social media, NeedsList-style marketplaces, state donation portals, 'please stop sending' notices
  SHORT: Donors send what they choose. Staff spend their labour sorting unneeded goods (the second disaster), and nobody answers each offer.
  VALUE: Each offer is accepted with a quantity and drop-off, or declined before it ships, and the needs decrement live. Donors see what their goods were used for.

- Coordination layer for a self-started helper hub [low] (V3, V7)
  WHO: Organisers of an emergent hub (Helfer-Shuttle, Superbergamo) coordinating hundreds to thousands of helpers from a WhatsApp group and a shared spreadsheet
  TODAY: WhatsApp groups, Google Sheets or Excel, paper, whiteboard, Facebook
  SHORT: The chat becomes unmanageable beyond about 50 members, a single phone operator becomes the bottleneck, and matching happens by calling out at morning roll call.
  VALUE: Messages posted in the chat they already use become commitments against the day's needs, and organisers handle only the exceptions.

- Freshness and triage for host-accommodation offers [low] (V3, V5)
  WHO: A matching team at an NGO or council handling tens of thousands of offers to host evacuees or refugees (for example #UnterkunftUkraine)
  TODAY: Web registration forms plus manual matching by partner organisations and councils
  SHORT: About 360,000 beds were offered and more than 60,000 people placed by September 2022. There was a backlog, hosts heard nothing, and offers went stale.
  VALUE: Instant acknowledgement, periodic confirm-or-withdraw checks and a triage queue. Safeguarding rules out automatic binding, so every placement still needs human vetting.

- Capability-targeted callouts for a pre-registered reserve [low] (V5)
  WHO: A national Red Cross society or civil-protection agency running a reserve that is alerted by SMS (Team Österreich, British Red Cross Community Reserve)
  TODAY: Online registration and regional SMS alerts with a Ja/Nein reply; admin filter-and-text in Better Impact or Rosterfy
  SHORT: Alerts go out by region, not by what people can bring. No evidence found that operators feel this as a problem.
  VALUE: Ask only the people whose self-described capability fits (chainsaw certificate, tractor, language), with consent and cooldown.

- Drafting needs from survivor requests (the missing input to matching) [low] (V1, V3)
  WHO: A VOAD or NGO intake desk turning survivor calls, paper notes and messages into work orders
  TODAY: Crisis Cleanup hotline with volunteer operators typing work orders; paper and email (Helfer-Shuttle)
  SHORT: Needs arrive on paper and by phone and change daily. Without written needs, offers cannot be matched, and in the early Ahr response nobody tried to decide what helpers would do.
  VALUE: Needs drafted from free text and confirmed by a person would feed the offer matcher. This is not built; it sits closer to V1.

## NOT WORTH IT
- Routine scheduled volunteering (food-bank shifts, events, school sign-ups): Each offer is the same thing, one person for one shift, so forms and slots work. Cheap mature tools (SignUpGenius, VolunteerLocal, Timecounts, Better Impact/Galaxy, Rosterfy) already send reminders and targeted messages. UK barriers are time, fit and flexibility, not the way the offer is made.
- Skills-based volunteering (Catchafire-style): Project templates and platform matching average about 5 days. The real work is scoping the project, not reading offers.
- Unskilled-labour surges where every offer is 'a pair of hands': Valencia placed more than 14,000 volunteers with a form and ticketed bus seats. Parsing free text adds little when the offers do not differ.
- Mass national volunteer registers (NHS Volunteer Responders type): The bottleneck was a shortage of tasks (about 600,000 approved against about 250,000 tasks by June 2020, from a search snippet), not intake. Faster intake would only make the oversupply bigger.
- Selling on no-show reduction: Every incumbent already automates reminders, and the only figures found are uncited vendor claims.
- Business case built on '30–50 relationships per coordinator': No evidence found. Published ratios range from 1:15 to 1:80 depending on context, and practitioners say there is no standard.
