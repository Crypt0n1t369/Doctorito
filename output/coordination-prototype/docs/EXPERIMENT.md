# One question, a useful account, a visible contribution

29 September 2026. Proposed experiment, not a running pilot. No participants have been recruited, no outreach sent and no public release authorized. The indicative duration is ten days after a willing lead is found. This is a workflow experiment, not a statistical trial or a new landing page.

## Start here

Use the [pilot workbook](experiments/WORKBOOK.md) to record the agreed question, observation window, roles, baseline, contributions, receipts and outcome. It is a manual worksheet, not running software. Keep participant identities and private observations in an appropriately restricted location; this repository is not a permission boundary.

## The hypothesis

A person or small group can maintain an inspectable account of a real question, accept useful contributions and resume the work with less reconstruction and tolerable review effort. Test the whole journey before adding a community, automated judging or funding machinery.

Candidate: [one Latvian municipal project](experiments/LATVIA.md), using public documents. Alternative: one maker's reproducible build or a team's recurring handover. Choose by access to a willing lead and a useful outcome. The candidate source research is not evidence that anyone wants the software.

## People and output

One responsible lead, two contributors and one reader who has not seen the work. These are roles to recruit, not existing users. People choose a task according to present interest and available time, and may decline or suggest another question. A skill profile does not assign them work.

Produce a short account answering: what is the question, what can the sources establish, what remains uncertain, what changed, and what useful question comes next? Keep exact references and revision history accessible behind that account. Deliver it privately to the participants first. External publication is a separate deliberate act.

## Sequence

1. **Agree on one question and comparison.** Begin with the four substantive source pages already identified for the Latvia candidate, or the smallest sufficient packet for another question. Add evidence only to resolve a concrete question; 8–12 documents is a suggested upper bound, not a quota. The lead uses their ordinary shared document and task list to prepare a brief. Record setup, checking, reconstruction and correction minutes, including facilitator effort. Save the baseline output.
2. **Test individual continuity.** Use the proposed record workflow manually or in a minimal local implementation. Save sources, findings and open questions. After two days, ask the lead to resume without reading old chat and identify the next useful step. Record help needed and time spent.
3. **Test a contribution.** Each contributor takes one bounded question. Retain submission → review → accepted revision or reason for non-use → effect on the account → a receipt the contributor can find. Contributions can correct the framing rather than merely fill predefined needs.
4. **Test disagreement and correction.** Seek credible contrary evidence or an alternative explanation. Record whether it changes the account and why. Do not invent counterevidence. If none is found, mark this test incomplete. A deliberately fabricated mistake may be used only in a clearly separated test fixture.
5. **Test handover.** Give the fresh reader the account without a briefing. Can they locate a supported finding's source, identify an unresolved question, distinguish a report from an independent check, and find a useful next action? Save the actual errors and questions.
6. **Close the episode.** Resolve each submission with a visible disposition, deliver a versioned brief and explain remaining limits. Ask whether anyone chooses to use the workflow for a next question. Prompted testing is not voluntary adoption.

Only after this works should an authorized participant try one bounded outside invitation. Until intake and permissions are ready, the fresh reader can simulate that step; label the result as simulation. Do not count an institutional response or physical improvement as achieved by producing a brief.

Using the same evidence twice creates a learning advantage. Treat the baseline comparison as directional. If a second independent episode is available, reverse the order or use comparable subquestions; record remaining differences. Do not manufacture a percentage improvement from a tiny sample.

## What participants should see

The default view is the current account, unresolved questions and a few useful ways to help. Selecting a finding reveals its exact sources, limitations and changes. Each contribution has a stable link and a visible receipt. Chat can supply input, but it is never the only place to find the work.

Show plain labels such as “saved draft,” “submitted for review,” “accepted into the account” and “published version.” Keep visibility, review and publication separate in storage: saving is not checking, accepting is not publishing, and public source material does not make a participant's private draft public. The person must be able to tell where an item lives immediately after saving it.

## Minimum implementation contract

Use durable IDs for the inquiry, sources, item revisions, contributions and output versions. Store a source URL, publisher, publication/event/retrieval dates, passage locator and permitted snapshot or content hash where available. Record inaccessible sources explicitly. Preserve originals and label translations. Link revisions to supporting or challenging sources without overwriting the earlier account.

A review command writes the accepted revision, review reason and contributor receipt consistently and idempotently. Publication selects an explicit reviewed version and audience. Retrieval, summaries and model requests enforce the recipient's grant before selecting context. A revoked source must not remain usable through a cached summary. C1–C9 remain authoritative; these requirements are not yet implemented merely because this document names them.

The existing matcher can serve a known need. Keep inquiry and review records distinct from commitments and model judgments. Do not infer a reviewed claim from a fulfilled task.

## What to measure and when to stop

Record counts and minutes: received, accepted, declined, awaiting review and closed contributions; unresolved factual errors; questions the fresh reader cannot answer; setup and upkeep time; source-checking time; correction effort; voluntary reuse. Keep unmeasured values null. Under twenty cases report counts, not rates.

Continue to a second episode only if a contribution materially improves the output, every submission receives a disposition, the fresh reader can explain the state and find its evidence, and someone chooses to reuse the workflow. These are proposed practical gates, not validated universal thresholds.

Simplify if ordinary documents produce equally inspectable results with less total effort. Stop adding contributors if review becomes an accumulating backlog. Suspend an affected path on audience leakage, unsupported attribution or unapproved release. Do not add funding, training or federation to rescue a failed core loop.

## Models and scope

No model key is required for the manual baseline. General LLMs may propose extraction, translation, questions and explanations with source references; a person reviews the result. Jev is an optional later test for bounded routing, relevance or duplicate suggestions, benchmarked separately on Latvian inputs. Neither model establishes truth or grants authority. Permissions, version control, arithmetic and release decisions stay explicit.

Use public or sanitized material in ordinary tools for the first manual trial. A prototype trial requires repair and verification of the boundaries it uses; private contributions still require Gate 0. Training local models waits for an observed repeated task, appropriate data rights and evidence that retrieval or simpler methods are insufficient.
